## The brief

Some projects go exactly to plan. This one went better, because the plan changed.

Stacey Spencer has spent more than 12 years in HR and recruitment across local government and private business, in both Australia and the UK. Now based in Perenjori, she is building [The Regional HR Consultancy](https://www.theregionalhrconsultancy.com.au/) to bring practical, people-first HR support to regional businesses.

She needed a full brand: a foundation that would carry through everything from her core identity to her website and social media. The scope included:

- A brand manifesto capturing the emotional intent behind the business
- Logo design
- A colour palette
- A font library
- A tone of voice guide
- A social media framework

## Starting with the why

Every brand we build starts with a manifesto, because a logo is only as strong as the idea behind it. For The Regional HR Consultancy, that idea arrived quickly and clearly: Making HR More Human.

It captures what Stacey believes – that where you live shouldn’t determine the quality of HR support you receive and working in the regions is different.

In regional WA, businesses often operate without the HR resources that their city counterparts take for granted. When things go wrong (such as a difficult dismissal, a compliance issue or a toxic team dynamic) there's rarely someone in-house to turn to. The impact of getting HR wrong in a small regional community doesn't stay within four walls; it ripples out into the street, the school, the sporting club.

This is where the brand started. The logo, colour palette, language and identity flowed from this one insight.

## Where we started

We developed an initial logo and font direction. We both liked it, we agreed to move forward, and on paper we were done with the creative stage.

Then Stacey paused. The project went quiet for a couple of months over school holidays, and this gave Stacey time to reflect. She wasn't convinced the concept truly represented her, or the direction she wanted for the business. It looked too polished.

Once we talked it through, the problem was clear. The design felt corporate. It could have belonged to any HR firm in any capital city. What it didn't say was regional, and it didn't say Stacey.

This is the part of the story I love most.

Stacey was brave enough to say that something wasn’t sitting right. Far too many people accept something they don’t love for fear of hurting another person’s feelings or feeling ‘silly’. The truth is that there is deep wisdom in asking to re-look at something. It demonstrates courage, intelligence and integrity (which, not surprisingly, is one of Stacey’s business values) and it sounds exactly like someone you would want working with you on human resources.

So, that was it. We went back to the drawing board. We simply couldn’t put something out into the market that Stacey didn’t wholeheartedly believe in.

## What changed, and what didn't

There were things in the brand kit that still strongly resonated. The manifesto and values stayed, because “Making HR More Human” was right. The colour palette and the language stayed too. Those foundations were solid.

What we rebuilt were the logo and the fonts, the two elements carrying the corporate feel.

The new direction is warmer, softer, and distinctly more regional. It feels approachable rather than imposing. You can actually feel a bit of Stacey in it, which is exactly what a personal-service consultancy needs. When clients see the brand, they are getting a sense of the person they will be working with.

## The ripple effect

A brand that feels right makes everything downstream easier. The social media rollout, the website and the day-to-day content all flowed more naturally because the brand aligns so well with who Stacey is. There's no stretching to fit, and no translating a corporate look into a human voice. The tone of voice guide sounds like her, the visuals feel like her, and the social framework gives her a way to show up consistently without feeling like she's performing.

## The lesson: don't be afraid to ask

There is real wisdom in asking for a do-over.

Many small business owners feel pressure to stick with a decision once it's been made, especially when they've already said yes. They worry about being difficult, or about wasting time and money.

The reality is the opposite. A brand is something you live with every day. It represents you long after the project is finished. Speaking up at the right moment saves far more than it costs.

Small business gets a better result when it feels right. Stacey trusted her instincts, and the outcome is a brand that is authentic, regional and human.

If something about your brand isn't sitting right, ask the question. It might be the best decision you make.
